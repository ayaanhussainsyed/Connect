02 Problem Statement
What business or technical problem should be solved? Describe the context, why it matters, and the main
pain points.

  Fonecta Connect helps local businesses get found online. It keeps their information – opening hours,
  photos, reviews – accurate and up to date in 35+ channels such as Google, Apple Maps and Bing. The
  same Nordic SaaS runs in Sweden as Hitta Connect and will soon launch in Norway.
  Today the customer logs in and does everything themselves: business details, photos, review replies and
  exception hours. Many forget or have no time. Search results then show old information, and their
  customers walk to a closed door.
  Connect is changing direction. It follows each customer’s situation, notices when something needs doing
  and sends a ready-made suggestion. The customer approves it with one click, and Connect makes the
  change.
  Your task: build a working demo of Connect contacting a customer – “Update your opening hours
  like this.” The demo must show the whole chain:
   1 Detect. How does Connect conclude that the opening hours need to change? Which data does it use?
   2 Suggest. Which hours does Connect propose, and why exactly those?
   3 Contact. The customer gets a message (email, SMS, WhatsApp or another channel) that states the
     suggestion and the reason clearly.
   4 Decide. The customer approves, edits or declines. No login needed.
   5 Apply. The approved change is written to Connect’s backend (Yext API), and the customer gets a
     confirmation of where the new hours now show.

03 Guiding Question
One question that focuses the challenge. Example: “How might we use AI to predict equipment failures before
they happen?”

  How might Connect use AI to notice when a business’s opening hours are about to be wrong,
  propose the right hours with a clear reason, and get the owner’s approval in one tap – so that
  no customer walks to a closed door?




04 Recommended AI Approaches Optional
Any relevant technologies, models or methods — for example NLP for text analysis, classification, knowledge
graphs, agents, or RAG.

   • Pattern learning: last year’s exceptions, the customer’s own past decisions and what similar
     businesses nearby do.
   • Rules + ML with confidence: send only when confident; otherwise ask, or stay quiet.
   • Visibility-aware priority: use search and view statistics to decide which suggestions to send first.
   • NLP / LLMs: read reviews (“closed although Google said open”) and write short, clear messages in
     Finnish, Swedish, Norwegian and English.
   • Learning from feedback: approve / edit / decline improves the next suggestion; standing permission
     for recurring changes.
   • Guardrails: the customer has the last word, nothing is published without approval or standing
     permission, and every suggestion keeps its reasons (audit trail).

06 Evaluation Criteria
How should solutions be judged? What matters most — for example accuracy, scalability, usability, or
business impact?

   • Suggestion quality: Is the suggestion sensible and backed by data? The scoring script measures it
     on hidden test data.
   • Customer experience: Does a busy entrepreneur understand the message at once, and is approving
     easy?
   • Reliability: Does the customer always have the last word? How are wrong suggestions prevented?
   • Extensibility: Which other, similar use cases would the same loop serve? (Automatic posts and
     review replies are already in production or on the roadmap.)
   • Learning: Does Connect learn from the customer’s decisions, so that the next suggestion is better
     after an edit or a decline? Can recurring changes get standing permission – “Handle public holidays
     from now on without asking”?
  The demo must show all five steps. A complete chain beats a perfect single step.
